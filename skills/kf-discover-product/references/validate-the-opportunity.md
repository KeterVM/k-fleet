# Validate the opportunity

Choose a probe that could change the next decision. Write the claim, the relevant
audience, the observation that would support or weaken it, and a bounded duration
or resource limit. Use existing adequate evidence first. Do not choose a sample
size, conversion target, or spending limit without a defensible task-specific basis.

Match the probe to the uncertainty:

- Problem uncertainty: observe or investigate current attempts and consequences.
- Comprehension or usability uncertainty: observe representative tasks with a
  prototype that exposes the uncertain interaction.
- Value or adoption uncertainty: examine actual use, repeated use, switching, or
  other behavior that bears on the promised benefit.
- Commercial uncertainty: investigate the buying process and authorized evidence
  of willingness to pay, rather than equating signups with purchases.

A feasibility spike establishes something about implementation, not desirability.
A working prototype does not prove adoption. A landing-page response does not prove
retention or viable economics. Explain the limit of the selected evidence.

Prepare the smallest useful probe within scope. Before an external action whose
authority is missing, make the exact action and materials reviewable and identify
the missing authorization. Do not repeat approval requests for an already authorized
probe or silently add outreach, charges, or tracking to a research-only task.

Compare observations with the original claim, including negative cases and selection
bias. Stop or change the probe when it answers the question, its premise fails, the
agreed limit is reached, or it cannot produce valid evidence. Report inconclusive
results honestly. Preserve the distinction between evidence supporting a next step
and proof of general product success.
