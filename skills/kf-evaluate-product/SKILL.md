# Evaluate product

Determine what a product is achieving and which next decision the evidence supports.
Use for requested outcome assessment, unexplained adoption or task-completion
problems, or evaluation of a product change. Passing acceptance tests establishes
behavior, not product value; skill effectiveness is a separate subject owned by
`kf-evolve-skills`.

## Select supporting guidance

- For outcome definitions, instrumentation, cohorts, or unreliable data, read
  [Measurement](references/measurement.md).
- For conflicting feedback, change effects, experiments, or a next-step decision,
  read [Interpretation and decisions](references/interpretation-and-decisions.md).

## Scope and authority

Use the user's intended outcome and current product commitments. Keep observed
results, inferred explanations, and preferences distinct. Ask when missing intent
changes what success means, the audience, or the decision; wait before dependent
work and continue independent checks. Do not redefine success to fit available data.

Analyze only authorized data within the relevant project, population, and period.
Use proportionate aggregation and protect sensitive records. Evaluation does not
automatically authorize new tracking, user contact, live experiments, pricing changes,
or product retirement. Carry existing authorization into requested implementation
and follow-through without adding a routine approval stop.

## Establish what the evidence can answer

Identify the decision, expected benefit, affected users, and time needed for that
benefit to become observable. Choose measures that could expose failure as well as
success. Separate product outcomes from proxies such as traffic or clicks, and
include relevant adverse effects and operating costs when they change the decision.

Inspect the measurement path before interpreting results: event meaning, population,
denominators, missing or duplicated records, timing, and changes in collection. Reuse
adequate instrumentation; a full analytics platform is not a prerequisite. Where
evidence is absent, report the unanswered question and the smallest useful authorized
way to resolve it, rather than supplying invented metrics.

Combine relevant quantitative evidence with actual user feedback or observations.
Investigate meaningful differences between groups and workflow stages without
generalizing from a selected sample. Distinguish association from a credible causal
effect; an improvement after release may have other explanations.

Choose further investigation or experiments only when they can change a material
decision. Agree on the question and defensible interpretation before acting, with
applicable limits for cost, exposure, and duration. Do not require an A/B test when
available traffic, randomization, or outcome delay makes it unsuitable.

## Close the product feedback loop

Report the supported result, relevant population and period, evidence quality,
material uncertainty, and next action. Recommend retaining, changing, investigating,
or stopping an approach in proportion to the evidence. Identify what observation
would overturn the recommendation; inconclusive evidence is a valid result.

Route the supported problem to the affected decision: value assumptions, requirements,
experience, code, or operation. Continue authorized corrections and relevant checks;
an assessment-only task remains read-only. Evidence that a goal should change is a
proposal for the user, not permission to change it.

Stop when evidence supports the requested decision or cannot do so with available
data and authority. State pending observations and ownership. Do not claim future
benefits are realized or imply ongoing measurement without an authorized mechanism.
